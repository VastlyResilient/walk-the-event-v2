# Decisions log

Format: decision · alternatives tried · why.

1. **Inputs differ from the brief.** Videos arrived as `DOWNSTAIRS.mp4` / `UPSTAIRS.mp4`: already re-encoded to **8-bit H.264 yuv420p but still tagged BT.2020 / HLG (arib-std-b67)**, with no Apple metadata tracks, no DOVI record and no APAC track. The mascot drawing `hermes-guide.png` was **not supplied**. No write-up text files: the cards exist as photos inside four of the six station folders.
2. **Tone mapping = Apple AVFoundation (VideoToolbox) HLG→BT.709.** This ffmpeg build has no `zscale`/`libplacebo`, so the brief's ffmpeg route is unavailable. We tried (a) naive ffmpeg decode, which reproduced the v1 failure (washed, shifted; mean error 25/255 vs reference), and (b) `bin/apple_tonemap` (Swift: `AVAssetReader` output with BT.709 colour properties), which matched the QuickTime/`AVAssetImageGenerator` reference stills to 1.5/255 mean. (b) is the same renderer Photos/QuickTime use, so it is the reference look by construction. Winner: (b). An analytic HLG LUT was not needed.
3. **HDR variant: skipped.** The source is 8-bit HLG, so a 10-bit HEVC HDR tier would add no real highlight detail and would double the media. SDR BT.709 is the only tier.
4. **Mascot reference generated.** Because the drawing was missing, we generated an on-model turnaround (front, ¾, side, back) with `google/gemini-3-pro-image` from the brief's exact description (`input/mascot/hermes-guide-turnaround.png`). `openai/gpt-image-2` returned 404 on OpenRouter.
5. **Budget caps lowered.** The OpenRouter key had ~$46 of credit left, so caps are $40 total / $25 video (see `docs/SPEND.md`).
6. **Watching the footage.** Frames at 2 fps (239 downstairs, 259 upstairs) were reviewed as labelled contact sheets (`work/sheets/`), producing `data/beats_*.json` with chapters. Whisper large-v3-turbo found **no intelligible speech** (crowd and music only; its text is hallucinated filler), so audio is used as ambience only.
7. **Breakdown ↔ station mapping.** Six screenshots ↔ six station folders, matched by label (exact, apart from case and "TVs"/"TVS"). Frames were located by thumbnail NCC plus SIFT/RANSAC: LED 16.65 s, TVs 39.54 s, Projector 48.15 s, Space 64.46 s, Merch 83.02 s, CPU 91.09 s, all verified visually. **TVs** points off-frame (left of the curved walkway), so it's photo-only and gets an edge beacon.
8. **Station facts.** Four stations have photographed wall cards (transcribed verbatim). Space Screen and Merch have **no card**, so their text describes only what is visible on screen or printed on shirts, and this is labelled in the UI ("Source: …").
9. **Stabilisation.** No gyro metadata survived and there is no libvidstab, so we use KLT + RANSAC similarity, Gaussian-smoothed (σ = 18 frames), with a 7% crop; corrections are ~10 px. Gyroflow was not usable.
10. **Colour bug found and fixed in QA.** OpenCV decodes H.264 with BT.601 while our masters are BT.709, so every OpenCV round trip shifted colour (ΔE 3 to 5). Colour-path reads now go through ffmpeg (`pipeline/vread.py`), re-rendered with the *same* stabilisation matrices so the solve and tracks stay valid.
11. **Scrubbing.** WebCodecs `VideoDecoder` + mp4box demux, decoding whole closed GOPs of 10 (no B-frames) into a bounded VideoFrame cache, drawn to a canvas that three.js samples. Fallback: `<video>` seeking on the same files. Colour, depth and person-mask are **packed into one frame** (`[colour | depth/mask]`), so they can never drift out of sync. 10 s segments; 720p tier (~2.1 Mbps) on phones, 1080p tier on desktop.
12. **Depth + masks.** Depth Anything V2 Base (MPS) at 15 fps, interpolated to 30, with temporally smoothed normalisation; SegFormer-B2 ADE20K "person" masks. SAM 2 / Video Depth Anything were skipped: heavier, no material win at this resolution.
13. **Camera solve.** pycolmap incremental at 6 fps (CLAHE copies), plus 10 fps re-solves of the gaps. Fragments are merged on temporal overlap (rotation averaging + LS scale/translation); where they don't overlap, each is levelled on its own floor and chained at the seam. Metric scale comes from a 1.40 m camera height. Downstairs is 84% solved (the dark outro after 100 s is unsolvable); upstairs is 97%. Per-frame depth calibration `1/z = a·d + b` against solved points drives occlusion.
14. **Hermes guide = procedural rigged toon model, not image→3D.** No Meshy/Tripo/Rodin key, no Blender, and Mixamo needs an account (not allowed). The character is built in three.js from the turnaround: a 19-bone rig, IK legs with distance-driven phase (**exact foot-lock**: phase += distance·duty/stride), knife-pleated striped skirt that deforms around the knees, painted manga face with blinks/expressions, toon ramp + inverted-hull ink outlines + footage-tinted rim light. Exported as a 1.2 MB GLB (~16k tris) with 20 baked clips (`public/media/guide/hermes-guide.glb`).
15. **Station pods = Apple SHARP splats.** Single hero photo → 1.18M Gaussians → adaptive 45–50% decimation → SPZ v3 (7.2–7.9 MB each), rendered with Spark. They hold up at ±12° (orbit clamped there), and the photo crossfades into the splat at the identical pose. **Licence:** SHARP weights are research/non-commercial; this site is a free, non-commercial fan recap and the credits say so. For commercial use, swap to the photo-parallax fallback (one line in `pod.ts`).
16. **V4 /lab World Mode: cut.** Marble needs an account; a splat trained from a forward-walking phone video produces the "scan" look v1 was rejected for. `/lab` shows a short explanation and is not linked from the UI.
17. **Ask Hermes.** `server/server.mjs` proxies `nousresearch/hermes-4-405b` (fallback: hermes-3-70b), grounded only in stations + chapters; tested live. The static site uses it when `VITE_ASK_URL` is set; otherwise a local grounded answer engine (keyword/alias matching over station cards) answers, so the drawer always works and no key ever reaches the client.

26. **Second guide version: Bobby's sculpted Meshy model (now the default).** Bobby supplied `model.glb` (a Meshy image-to-3D sculpt, 636k tris) and a Meshy key. Pipeline (`pipeline/meshy.py`, `tools/gltf/build-meshy.mjs`): decimate to 140k → Meshy **rigging** (1.6 m humanoid, 24 bones) → Meshy **animations** in two merged sets (idle, walking woman, run, walk-turn, climb stairs, look-around, happy sway; big wave, wave, happy jump, cheer, two talk gestures, call, big heart) → clips moved onto the rig with the hips' forward drift removed (the guide brain owns root motion) → 24.5k tris, WebP 1K texture, meshopt: **1.35 MB**.
    - **Auto-rig fixes at load time** (`reweightSkirt`): Meshy bound the long skirt to the thighs and to the hands, and the short sleeves to the spine. Strides tore the skirt, raised arms dragged it out like wings, and sleeves froze while arms moved. The skirt is re-weighted to the hips (continuous by distance from the leg bones: 100% at the waist, 55% at the hem), arm influence is stripped from cloth away from the arms, and the sleeves are bound to the upper arms. The classification is geometric, because painted pleat shadows broke texture-based tests.
    - **Hair and cloth flow**: GPU secondary motion after skinning. Hair (dark texels above the neck, face excluded) lags her velocity and sways; the skirt lags, flares with the stride phase and swirls.
    - Meshy's "Happy Jump" is a 10 s loop, so only the first hop plays. One-shot clips release their weight when finished. With no "point" preset, she presents stations with the open-hands talk gesture; procedural arm aiming fought this rig.
    - The illustrated toon guide stays available: Settings → Guide look, or `?guide=toon`.
27. **Mobile performance pass (v2.1).** Phones were getting the 1080p packed tier (3.1 MP per frame, decoded in whole GOPs) and holding up to 30 decoded frames, every frame was drawn into a 2D canvas and then re-uploaded to WebGL, the tag overlay and speech bubble forced a synchronous layout every frame, and the walk kept rendering underneath an open pod (two WebGL contexts at once). Now: phones and tablets default to 720p (1080p stays in Settings); the decoded `VideoFrame` goes straight to the GPU as a plain `THREE.Texture` (not `VideoFrameTexture`, which three.js uploads as linear RGBA8 and would wash the colours out; a plain texture keeps the sRGB8 hardware decode and allocates storage once; measured identical to the canvas path, mean abs diff 0.02/255), with a one-time feature test that falls back to the canvas; a smaller frame cache on phones; adaptive pixel ratio (≤1.6 on phones, steps down when frames run over 24 ms, back up when there's headroom); tag sizes cached and the bubble measured once per line; no walk render while a pod covers the screen; the pod loop runs on real time (same motion at 30/60/120 Hz).
28. **Station switching.** The splat renderer and Spark's shaders are warmed the first time any station comes into view, and the hero photo is pre-decoded on approach, so the first open doesn't hitch. The gallery no longer closes to black and re-opens: the next photo is decoded first, then slide-crossfades over the current one while the splat fades out and back in when ready; neighbours' photos are pre-decoded.
29. **Scene sense: the guide reads the footage.** A 36×64 grid of depth, people mask and colour is rendered from the packed frame into a tiny render target and read back asynchronously (`readRenderTargetPixelsAsync`, ~7 Hz, no stall), with the camera of the frame it came from. A spot is rejected if the floor under her feet isn't visible at the depth it should be (a rail, plinth or wall in front of her feet, or a drop/window behind), if something solid is in front of her body, or if a person is where she'd stand; people are unprojected to floor positions and she steers around them and avoids targets whose straight path crosses them. Locomotion is now human: she walks where she faces, turns before setting off, accelerates ~2.6 m/s² and stops ~4 m/s², slows through turns, then turns to face the visitor once she arrives; a sticky target stops her shuffling between near-equal spots.
30. **Room light on her body.** The same readback gives the footage colour around her upper body and under her feet: the hemisphere light's sky colour/strength follows the room around her, its ground colour follows the floor bounce, the key light follows local brightness, and the rim takes the room's hue. She goes pink in the red corridor, blue in the blue rooms, dims in dark corners and flickers with the corridor lights.
31. **Presenting, racing, dancing.** Meshy has no pointing preset, so the point is procedural: after the mixer, the upper arm and forearm are swung (world-space, axis-agnostic) toward the station, aimed in the screen plane (an arm aimed at a work beyond her would point straight away from the camera and vanish behind her), with her body turned 80 % toward the visitor; she keeps presenting while the visitor lingers. Upstairs, a `race` state in the Long Corridor (47–63 s) sprints her ~8.5 m ahead, she waits and waves big (Meshy "Wave for Help") until you're within 3 m, then races again; on the Disco Floor she dances whenever she stands (three Meshy dances, hips pinned so she dances on her spot) and walks with a quicker cadence. Lines for all three personalities. Also fixed a state-machine bug where outside the dark passage she never returned to follow/idle (an `else if` chain hung off the dark-zone check), which had also silenced the upstairs room lines.

33. **Second review round: rig and placement fixes.** (a) The auto-rig re-weighting computed joint positions with `mesh.bindMatrixInverse`, which three.js overwrites every frame in `attached` mode; with the armature's 0.01 scale every joint landed ~100× off, so the arm/sleeve logic silently never ran and her forearms and hands were welded to her hips (any raised-arm clip just stretched the sleeve: the "touching her face" wave). Joints now come from `bindMatrix⁻¹ · boneInverse⁻¹`. (b) In the sculpt her hands rest on, and are fused into, the skirt, so lifted hands stretched cloth into sheets; at load, every triangle below the chest that straddles hand skin and skirt cloth is given wholly to the skirt (its hand-side corners duplicated with skirt weights), so hands lift cleanly. (c) Pointing: a 20–50°-below-horizontal presenting arm (a horizontal arm still stretches the armpit of an arms-down rig), open stance facing the visitor, plus a fine pink **pointer beam** with a travelling spark from her hand to the station's glow or edge beacon, so the gesture reads instantly on a phone. A full horizontal point would need the model regenerated in an A-pose. (d) Clips that shouldn't travel have their hips pinned to the rig's rest position (Meshy's walk-turn swings the hips 1.3 m mid-clip). (e) Placement: hand-placed nav blockers for the black cube bench (monocular depth reads it as floor) and the balcony curb, which she also steers around; at the end of a walked path she stands beside the visitor instead of "ahead" over the balcony edge; arrival is a short settling step (no sideways slide or orbiting); the placement search runs ~6×/s with a cache invalidated by camera jumps. (f) Decode: GOPs are only marked complete when every frame is cached, the settle keeps re-requesting until the exact frame shows, and floor switches swap all floor state atomically.

35. **Mobile lag, round 2 (measured in iOS Simulator Safari with a `?perf` on-screen readout).** Two real causes: (a) the scene-sense GPU readback (`readRenderTargetPixelsAsync`) is non-blocking in Chrome but stalled Safari's main thread ~50 ms each time, several times a second: 7.7 ms/frame on average, the "freezes". The sense now shrinks the decoded frame off the main thread (`createImageBitmap` with resize to 108×128) and reads a tiny canvas (values converted to linear to match the shaders): Safari JS per frame while walking went from 11.2 ms avg / 36 ms max to 3.6 ms / 7 ms. (b) The guide was deliberately hidden whenever decoding lagged the scroll by more than 6 frames, so on a fast scroll she vanished; now the camera, guide and glows always follow the frame actually on screen, so she stays locked to the footage. The canvas upload path was re-measured and is slower in Safari (16 ms/frame), so the direct VideoFrame upload stays. `?perf`, `?noguide`, `?nosense`, `?nodirect` remain as diagnostics.

46. **Guide motion v4: she walks to spots on the measured lane, in frame (fixes "glides", "through walls", "larger", "on air at the end").** Diagnosed from a per-frame probe on the mobile profile at 4× CPU throttle (`qa/tools/guide_probe.py`, screenshots in the run notes) before touching the code. Root causes:
    - **Glides.** The lane follower moved her position straight at the lane point every frame (`maxStep`) independent of her facing; at slow scrolls (< 0.25 m/s lane speed) she faced the visitor while sliding along the lane, and side switches / `leadAlt` / the camera-front fallback slid her sideways: 25 % of visible upstairs frames and 31 % of downstairs slow-scroll lane frames moved > 0.15 m/s more than 45° off her facing.
    - **Through walls.** Lane candidates (`prefer ± 0.5`, `-prefer`) and the camera-front fallback never checked the nav map's measured free lane: 12–18 % of upstairs frames stood > 0.2 m outside it (inside the Plant Room planter, against the violet-gallery walls). The live footage check couldn't catch it because the scene sense read depth with the solve's feature-point fit, which in the tilted `conf 0.5` stretch (frames 2100–3300) puts the visible floor at **0.28–0.67×** its true distance (`qa/tools/sense_depth_check.py`): clear floor read as "something in front of her feet" ~half the time, so the checks were noise. The guide's occlusion shader used the same fit (and applied it to the linearised byte), cutting her as if walls stood in front of her there.
    - **Larger.** Her lead was measured along the path: at the upstairs U-turns 3.9 m of path is ~1 m from the camera (she filled 2–2.7× the screen height).
    - **On air at the end.** The last four upstairs frames (3868–3871, and the seam stretches 1416–1439, 2112–2177, 3501–3520) have `conf 0`, which switched her to the downstairs dark-passage "lantern" placement: 2.4 m in front of the camera = out over the atrium beyond the glass balustrade (footage floor 7–9× farther than her feet).
    - Also: the solve bridge at 3501–3518 moves the camera ~7 m in 0.6 s of footage; the lane follower chased it (355 m/s jumps).
